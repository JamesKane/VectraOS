# M6 — Runtime: step records

Step records, moved from [milestones.md](../milestones.md) on 2026-10-08, which keeps the synopsis. Each step's row is updated in the commit after it (a commit cannot name its own hash).

Scoped 2026-10-04: what the desktop, Mesa, `aid` and the toolchain stand on (04 §6), with the manual's tooling first (12 §11) and SIMD before threads. Decisions: x86 saves every XSAVE component the CPU has (AVX, AVX2, AVX-512); arm64 keeps NEON, with SVE and SME off (no tiered board has them; they come with the Sky1 if it is promoted); the real PC (T1) stays in M6, last. After a review of Jonathan Blow's critique of OS design (`a452c1a`, 2026-10-04): robust futexes are built in 6d (01 §13, question 6), protection keys are taken all the way in 6c (question 7), and 6e gives 01 §6.6's structured sharing the calls it rests on. A native C library was found missing while scoping a Swift port (2026-10-04): 6e2 adds the `<arch>-unknown-vectraos` target, with llvm-libc, libc++ and libc++abi from the toolchain as its runtime libraries (ADR-0033, amended 2026-10-06). Swift became a first-party language (ADR-0034, 2026-10-05), and `swift-on-vectra`'s requirements (`docs/os-requirements.md`, R1–R20) are slotted into 6d, 6e and 6e2, with 6e3 for Swift's three stages and `dbg`. Decided with them (2026-10-05): `exit(n)` is `n` in decimal; `libvx` gains symbolic links but no hard links; tzdata waits for M7; no POSIX names in `libvx`. The manual's backlog is written by agents in waves alongside the other steps, each wave reviewed and committed. After 6d5, every later step was split into chunks of a few days each (2026-10-06): 6d6–6d10, 6e1 (the old 6e, the runtime's APIs), 6e2, 6e3, 6f1 (the old 6f, the loader), 6f2 and 6g, each in lettered or numbered rows. A group's name, such as 6e1 or 6g, still names all its parts where other documents cite it.
